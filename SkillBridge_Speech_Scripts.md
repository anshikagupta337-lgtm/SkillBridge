## PART 1: ENGLISH SCRIPT

**Slide 1 – Title [A]**
Good morning, respected mentors and judges. We are Team Alpha Coders. I am Anshika Gupta, and this is Kartikey Verma. Today we are presenting SkillBridge, a simple website that helps students find the right first opportunity.

**Slide 2 – Problem [A]**
Let us start with a question. How many of us felt confused while looking for our first internship? Students often do not know which jobs match their skills. Job descriptions ask for skills we have not learned yet. And most of the time, we do not know what to learn next. This confusion stops many talented students from even applying.

**Slide 3 – Our Idea [K]**
So we built SkillBridge. It brings three steps into one place. First, the student chooses a job role and the skills they already have. Second, the website shows which skills are missing. Third, a simple roadmap tells them what to learn next. Our goal is not to promise a job. Our goal is to help students prepare.

**Slide 4 – Features [K]**
We kept the first version small and practical. It has five features: a student profile, skill matching, a skill-gap checker, a learning roadmap, and a list of sample internships.

**Slide 5 – Example [A]**
Let me explain with an example. A student knows HTML and CSS and wants to be a Frontend Developer. The job needs HTML, CSS and JavaScript. SkillBridge shows that two out of three skills match, which is about 67 percent, and tells the student that JavaScript is the next skill to learn. This score is based on listed skills only, and we are clear that it does not guarantee job suitability.

**Slide 6 – Tools [K]**
We used only HTML, CSS and JavaScript. HTML builds the pages, CSS makes the design clean and responsive, and JavaScript handles matching and results. It runs in any browser, with no paid API and no special setup.

**Slide 7 – Roadmap [A]**
We are a team of two. I lead the design and the student experience, and Kartikey leads the matching logic and roadmap. Today we have a working prototype. Next, we want to pilot it in our own college. Then we add verified vacancies, and in future, alumni mentors. Idea today, impact tomorrow.

**Slide 8 – Demo [K]**
Now let us show you one complete student journey. *(Switch to the website.)* We load the sample profile, choose Frontend Developer, and select HTML and CSS. Here is the match score and the missing JavaScript skill. Now the roadmap shows the learning steps. Finally, we can search the sample opportunities.

**Slide 9 – Why useful [A]**
For students, it means less guessing. For colleges, it can be a starting point for career guidance and mentoring. In future, we can add verified vacancies, real accounts and alumni mentors. Right now we use sample listings and simple rules, not live job data, and we are honest about that.

**Slide 10 – Close [A + K]**
**[K]** SkillBridge takes students from skills to a clearer next step: find a match, understand the gap, start learning.
**[A]** Thank you so much. We would love to hear your feedback and questions.

## Q&A Preparation (likely mentor questions)

**Q1. Is the data real?**
No. The first version uses sample listings and simple skill rules. We say this openly. Real vacancies are a future upgrade.
*(Hinglish: Nahi, abhi sample data hai. Real vacancies future upgrade hain.)*

**Q2. How is the match score calculated?**
Matched skills divided by required skills. In our example, 2 divided by 3 gives about 67 percent.
*(Hinglish: Matched skills ko required skills se divide karte hain. 2 by 3 = lagbhag 67%.)*

**Q3. What makes this different from LinkedIn or Internshala?**
Those sites list jobs. SkillBridge focuses on the gap between the student and the job, and tells them what to learn next.
*(Hinglish: Wo jobs dikhate hain. Hum skill gap dikhate hain aur batate hain ki aage kya seekhna hai.)*

**Q4. How will you earn or sustain this?**
Partnerships with colleges for placement cells, and later with learning platforms and companies for verified listings.
*(Hinglish: Colleges ke placement cells ke saath partnership, baad mein companies aur learning platforms ke saath.)*

**Q5. What is next?**
Verified vacancies, real user accounts, and alumni mentors.
*(Hinglish: Verified vacancies, real accounts aur alumni mentors.)*

## Delivery Tips
- Speak slowly and smile. Mentors remember confidence more than perfect words.
- Start the problem slide with the question, and pause for 2 seconds.
- Practise the demo twice beforehand. Keep a screenshot backup in case the internet or laptop fails.
- Be honest about limits (sample data). Judges respect honesty more than overclaiming.
- Finish within 5 minutes and keep a calm voice for Q&A.

### Extra ideathon Q&A
**Q. Who is your target user?**
First and second year college students looking for their first internship, and college placement cells.
*(Hinglish: Pehle aur dusre saal ke college students jo first internship dhundh rahe hain, aur college placement cells.)*

**Q. How will you reach users?**
Start with our own college, then spread through placement cells and student communities.
*(Hinglish: Pehle apna college, phir placement cells aur student communities.)*

**Q. What are your risks?**
Skill data must stay up to date, and the score is only a guide. That is why we always say it does not guarantee job suitability, and why verified data is on our roadmap.
*(Hinglish: Skill data update rakhna padega aur score sirf guide hai. Isliye hum clearly bolte hain ki ye job guarantee nahi hai.)*

### Ideathon pitching tips
- Open with the problem and one question. Do not start with technology.
- Keep one line stuck in their memory: **"Don't just search for jobs. Bridge the gap."**
- Spend 40 percent of your time on problem, idea and impact, and less on tools.
- Show the prototype for 30 to 60 seconds only. The idea is the hero.
- Share the speaking time: Anshika opens and closes, Kartikey covers the idea, tools and demo.
- Rehearse once with a timer. Aim for 4 minutes, so you have room for questions.
